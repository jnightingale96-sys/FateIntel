# US Legacy/Inorganic Contaminants — Regulatory & Model Matrix

Research scope: US federal (EPA) frameworks for assessing legacy/persistent and inorganic contaminants (PCBs, metals/metalloids, PAHs, legacy organochlorine pesticides, dioxins/furans, organotins, petroleum hydrocarbons/BTEX, chlorinated solvents/VOCs, cyanide, nutrients) and contaminated land/sediment/groundwater generally, for the purpose of scoping a FateIntel product expansion. Compiled 2026-09-19.

**Research method:** every row below is backed by a primary source (an EPA guidance document, EPA program webpage, the eCFR, or the Federal Register) that was actually opened via web search/fetch in this session. Where a source could only be verified through a secondary intermediary (e.g., a summarization pass on a PDF, or a third-party mirror like RAIS.ornl.gov) rather than direct primary text, that is flagged explicitly in the row or in the honesty section below. No content here was written from memory without a same-session source check.

## What's genuinely confirmed vs. what needs a follow-up session

**Solidly confirmed (primary EPA page/document opened and read, content cross-checked):**
- RAGS structure (Parts A, B, D, E, F) and ERAGS existence/status as "Interim Final" 1997 guidance — each part's own EPA landing page was fetched.
- RSLs are live, actively maintained, cover soil/air/tapwater/sediment/fish/groundwater, explicitly include PCBs (with TEFs), PAHs (with relative potency factors), TPH (with fraction-specific toxicity values), and metals — confirmed from the RSL User's Guide and RSL FAQ pages directly.
- RSL derivation as exposure-factor × toxicity-value equations — confirmed generally (standard intake-rate/body-weight/exposure-duration structure), though the exact algebraic form quoted by the fetch tool should be treated as illustrative, not a verbatim regulatory formula — a follow-up session should pull the literal equations from the "RSL Equations" page/User's Guide PDF directly rather than through a summarizing fetch.
- TSCA PCB regulation at 40 CFR Part 761, including the three cleanup pathways (self-implementing §761.61(a), performance-based §761.61(b), risk-based §761.61(c)) and the February 2024 amendment to §761.61(b) — confirmed via EPA fact sheet and eCFR.
- Framework for Metals Risk Assessment (2007) exists, was SAB peer-reviewed (2005/2006), and its core "metals ≠ organics" principles (persistence/non-destructibility, speciation-dependent toxicity, essentiality for some metals, natural background, and caution against generic BCF/BAF use) — confirmed from the EPA fact-sheet page; the full PDF itself could not be text-extracted in this session (see gaps below).
- Eco-SSLs: confirmed final values exist for 17 metals + 5 organic entries (DDT/metabolites, dieldrin, PAH-LMW, PAH-HMW, pentachlorophenol), four receptor groups (plants, soil invertebrates, mammals, birds), and the explicit EPA caveat that Eco-SSLs are screening values, **not** cleanup levels.
- ESBs (Equilibrium Partitioning Sediment Benchmarks) exist for PAH mixtures, ~32 other nonionic organics, and a metals-mixture model (Cd, Cu, Pb, Ni, Ag, Zn) — confirmed via EPA Science Inventory records.
- 2007 copper BLM-based freshwater AWQC is real, final, and confirmed as the national-recommended criterion; a marine/estuarine BLM was explicitly **not** finalized in 2007 because the saltwater BLM was judged not ready — confirmed via EPA water-quality-criteria pages and Federal Register notice.
- Lead is handled as a special case outside standard RSL toxicity-value math: IEUBK model for children, Adult Lead Methodology (ALM) for adults/non-residential, because EPA has no consensus oral RfD/slope factor for inorganic lead — confirmed via EPA's "Lead at Superfund Sites" pages.
- Dioxin/furan TEQ approach: EPA recommends 2005 WHO mammalian TEFs (per EPA's 2010 guidance, EPA/100/R-10/005) — confirmed via EPA risk-assessment page and the source PDF's own title/citation.
- TPH fraction-based risk methodology (TPHCWG approach; carbon-range fractions with surrogate toxicity values) is real and is the basis for EPA's PPRTV fraction toxicity values — confirmed via EPA PPRTV documents.
- CERCLA RI/FS process and the nine remedy-selection criteria (threshold/balancing/modifying) trace to the 1988 OSWER Directive 9355.3-01 — confirmed via EPA/OSTI citation and EPA's "Key Principles of Superfund Remedy Selection" page.
- Radiological risk assessment at Superfund sites is **integrated into**, not fully separate from, RAGS (RAGS Part A Ch. 10, RAGS Part B Ch. 4), supplemented by OSWER-specific directives (9200.4-18, 9200.4-40) — confirmed via EPA page fetch, though the OSWER directive numbers and specific dose-limit figures (12 mrem/yr ARAR framing, 25 mrem NRC decommissioning non-use) came through a summarizing fetch and should be verified against the primary directive PDFs before being hard-coded anywhere.
- 2015 OSWER Vapor Intrusion Technical Guide, and the separate Petroleum Vapor Intrusion technical guide (EPA 510-R-15-001), are real and confirm EPA treats chlorinated-solvent vapor intrusion and petroleum vapor intrusion as methodologically distinct (aerobic biodegradation attenuates petroleum vapors; chlorinated solvents do not attenuate the same way and produce toxic daughter products) — confirmed via direct EPA PDF titles/URLs.
- California DTSC/HERO issues its own Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) Notes and DTSC-SLs (target risk 1×10⁻⁶) as a state-level, nationally-referenced program distinct from EPA RSLs — confirmed via DTSC's own site, not deep-dived per instructions.

**Needs a dedicated follow-up session (do not treat as verified yet):**
- The literal RSL toxicity/exposure equations (exact variable definitions, e.g., whether current tables still use the "IUR" carcinogenic air formula reported here) should be pulled directly from the "Regional Screening Levels (RSLs) - Equations" EPA page/PDF, not reconstructed from a summarized fetch.
- Full text of the Framework for Metals Risk Assessment (2007) PDF was not machine-readable in this session (binary/compressed PDF; local `pdftoppm`/poppler not installed to render it). The fact-sheet-level summary used here is accurate but shallower than the full document — a follow-up should get the actual PDF text (e.g., via the archive.epa.gov mirror or OCR) for bioavailability/essentiality/background-concentration methodological detail (e.g., specific relative bioavailability adjustment methods for arsenic/lead).
- The full 8-step ERAGS process description in this document is sourced from a secondary mirror (RAIS.ornl.gov summary and an EPA Region 5 archived page), not a direct read of the primary 1997 ERAGS PDF (239 pages) itself — the step names are consistent between both secondary sources but should be checked against EPA 540-R-97-006 directly before being treated as canonical for product copy.
- No explicit EPA statement was found (in this session) that RSLs formally "supersede" the 1996/2002 Soil Screening Guidance; the two appear to coexist, with RSL tables citing SSG methodology as their basis. This relationship needs a clean primary citation rather than inference.
- Organotins (e.g., tributyltin) — **not researched in this session at all**. No EPA-specific organotin sediment/water framework was searched for; TBT is known to have EPA aquatic life criteria historically, but this needs its own search pass.
- Cyanide — **not researched in this session**. EPA has AWQC and RSLs for cyanide (implied by general RSL coverage) but no cyanide-specific methodology was verified here.
- Nutrients (N/P) — **not researched in this session**; this is a materially different assessment domain (eutrophication/TMDL-based, not toxicological) and was out of the search budget this pass.
- State-level frameworks beyond California DTSC (e.g., any other unusually influential state program) were not investigated, per the instruction to only flag existence, not build out.
- TRIM.FaTE "current status": the tool's own EPA page presents it as maintained and available for download, with mercury and dioxin as its two major historical validation case studies (2005 evaluation reports). However, no evidence was found in this session of *recent* (2020s) regulatory applications — its main validation/use record appears concentrated in the mid-2000s. This reads as a legacy-but-still-hosted tool rather than a tool in active regulatory rotation; treat "current status" as **available but not confirmed to be in routine current use** pending a follow-up search specifically for recent (post-2015) TRIM.FaTE applications or successor tools.
- AWQC for the full metals suite (arsenic, chromium III/VI, lead, mercury, nickel, selenium, silver, zinc) beyond copper was only searched at a summary level; each metal's specific current criterion document, and the 2023 court vacatur of the 2016 cadmium chronic freshwater criterion (reverting to the 2001 criterion), was found but not deep-verified per-metal.

## Not researched this session (explicit gaps, do not assume coverage)

- Organotins (e.g., tributyltin) — no search performed.
- Cyanide — no search performed.
- Nutrients (nitrogen/phosphorus, eutrophication/TMDL assessment) — no search performed; this is a structurally different (non-toxicological) assessment paradigm and likely deserves its own research pass.
- Legacy organochlorine pesticides beyond DDT/dieldrin (e.g., chlordane, toxaphene, aldrin/endrin) were not individually verified beyond their appearance in the Eco-SSL contaminant list.
- Per-metal AWQC vintage/status beyond copper and the cadmium vacatur was not individually verified for arsenic, chromium, lead, mercury, nickel, selenium, silver, zinc.
- Groundwater-specific modeling tools (e.g., EPA's BIOSCREEN, REMChlor for chlorinated solvent plumes) were not researched at all in this session, despite being highly relevant to the "contaminated groundwater assessment generally" part of scope.
